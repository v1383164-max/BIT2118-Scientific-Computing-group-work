BIT 2118 SCIENTIFIC COMPUTING - GROUP WORK SUMMARY (for the presentation)

WHAT WAS DONE
For every task (2 to 20) we did four things:
  1. Ran the given program and identified its inputs and the maths it performs.
  2. Changed one input or parameter and explained how the result changed.
  3. Explained the output (and the graph) in plain words.
  4. Wrote a new program for a different but related real-world case, with its own assumptions, data, output and interpretation.
All programs are in the folders code, changed_parameter and modified_case. All outputs and graphs are in outputs. A full write-up per task is in docs.

TASK BY TASK (original idea -> what we changed -> our new real-world case)
 2  NumPy arrays: daily temperature stats. Changed one reading to a spike: mean and max rose, min did not. New case: water-tank sensor levels.
 3  Vectorization: 1 million latencies, about 56x faster than a loop. Fewer values gave a smaller speed-up. New case: 2 million prices with currency and VAT, about 68x faster.
 4  Floating-point errors: 0.1 + 0.2 is not exactly 0.3, so use np.isclose. A better approximation of sqrt(2) cut the error about 60 times. New case: adding a million small bank deposits.
 5  Matrix algebra: rotate and scale a square, determinant 3 is the area scaling. Changing 45 to 90 degrees turned it a quarter turn. New case: drone waypoints with rotation, scaling and shifting.
 6  Linear systems: 3 Type A and 4 Type B servers. New totals gave 5 and 3. New case: traffic flows of 300, 500 and 200 vehicles per hour.
 7  LU decomposition: factor once, solve many times. A different coefficient changed the answers. New case: circuit currents, LU about 46 times faster for repeated solves.
 8  Iterative methods: Gauss-Seidel needs about half the iterations of Jacobi. A looser tolerance meant fewer iterations but less accuracy. New case: heat along a 7-point rod.
 9  Eigenvalues: nodes B and C are the most central. Adding a link made all nodes equal. New case: PageRank of a 5-page website.
10  Bisection and fixed point: equilibrium temperature 27.53 C. A stronger heat term gave 29.56 C. New case: break-even at 76.4 units.
11  Newton and secant: IRR of 11.54 percent. Bigger final cash flow gave 17.09 percent. New case: loan interest rate of 1.56 percent per month.
12  Nonlinear equations: circle meets parabola at two points. A bigger circle moved the points. New case: drone path against a no-fly zone, plus projectile landing time.
13  Interpolation: cubic spline is smoother than linear. New case: filling missing sensor data; cubic error was about 60 times smaller.
14  Curve fitting: quadratic model of server delay. Predicting further out (500 users) is less reliable. New case: app user growth, exponential model fits far better than a line.
15  Numerical differentiation: speed from GPS positions. One bad reading spoiled nearby estimates. New case: acceleration from velocity; central difference is more accurate than forward.
16  Numerical integration: energy from power readings, about 15.7 to 15.8 kWh. A sharper peak made the rules differ more. New case: river water volume; Simpson is about 100 times more accurate.
17  Euler method: cooling curve. A bigger step (5 min) made the error about 4 times larger. New case: logistic population growth.
18  solve_ivp: RC circuit charging. A bigger capacitor charges slower (3.57 V instead of 4.97 V at 5 s). New case: SIR epidemic, peak of 404 infected on day 27.
19  Optimization: cheapest server mix is all Type B. Doubling the requirement doubled the cost. New case: chairs and tables, best profit 1350.
20  Monte Carlo: server overload chance 10.6 percent. Higher demand raised it to 25.1 percent. New case: project deadline risk, 57 percent chance of missing 30 days.

MAIN LESSONS
- Vectorized NumPy code is far faster than loops.
- Computer arithmetic has tiny errors, so compare numbers with a tolerance.
- Direct methods give exact answers; iterative methods trade accuracy for speed.
- Smaller steps or tighter tolerances give better accuracy but cost more computation.
- Always check results: residuals, comparison with exact solutions, and graphs.
- Be careful when predicting far outside the measured data.
